The provisions that matter most for bail

Thirteen rules run on every assessment. These are the ones people ask about most.

Bail as of right

In bailable offences, bail is a right — not a favour.

BNSS s.478

Default bail (60/90 days)

Release if no charge sheet is filed within 60 or 90 days of remand.

BNSS s.187(3)

Anticipatory bail

Protection before arrest when a non-bailable offence is alleged.

BNSS s.482

Undertrial release

Release after half — or one-third for first-timers — of the maximum sentence.

BNSS s.479

Arrest safeguards

24-hour production, grounds of arrest and informing a relative.

BNSS ss.47, 48, 58

Free legal aid

A free lawyer for anyone who cannot afford one.

Questions

Frequently asked

Is this legal advice?

No. Bail Reckoner gives legal information. It shows which provisions may be relevant and why, but only a court decides bail. Please consult an advocate or your District Legal Services Authority.

Do I need an account?

No. Anyone can run an assessment. A free account lets you save assessments and open them again later.

Which law does it use?

The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS) and Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS), which replaced the CrPC and IPC on 1 July 2024. Old CrPC/IPC section numbers are shown alongside for reference.

How are the rules kept up to date?

Administrators propose changes, which only take effect after a second "verify & publish" step. Every change is logged, and each assessment lists the rule versions it used.

About

About Bail Reckoner

Many people in custody — and their families — don't know that bail may already be their right, or that a deadline has passed. Bail Reckoner turns the bail provisions of the BNSS into clear questions and explanations.
